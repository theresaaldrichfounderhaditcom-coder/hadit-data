How to read this

  • What an inspection covers. OIG inspectors visit VA health care systems on a cycle and review five areas: culture, environment of care, patient safety, primary care (now called integrated veteran care), and the veteran-centered safety net. Each report lists the recommendations the facility has to act on.
  • Zero recommendations is not a clean bill of health. It means OIG found nothing in the areas it reviewed that rose to a formal recommendation. Reports often describe problems a facility fixed on the spot or was already working on. Read the full report.
  • Open and closed are OIG's calls. A recommendation closes when OIG accepts the facility's proof that it was fixed. Open does not always mean overdue: each recommendation has its own target date, and some run a year or more. We check OIG's status every week.
  • The issue tags are HadIt's, applied to OIG's formal recommendations so you can compare facilities. "Repeat findings" means the report tied a problem to an earlier OIG, Joint Commission, or VISN review. The recommendation text is OIG's own.
  • Dates are publication dates. The site visit is usually several months earlier.
  • The OIG report is the source of record. If anything here disagrees with it, the report wins. Tell us at founder@hadit.com.

What you can do with it

If your VA has open recommendations, you can ask the facility's Patient Advocate what is being done about them. If you have seen a problem yourself, you can report it to the OIG Hotline at vaoig.gov/hotline.
